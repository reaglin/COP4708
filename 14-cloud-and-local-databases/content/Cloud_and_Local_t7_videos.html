Introduction

Choosing where a database lives is not only a question of cost or convenience — it is a question of how much downtime an organization can tolerate, how quickly it can recover when something fails, and who is accountable when data is exposed. This topic brings those concerns together under three headings: availability (will the database respond when users need it), reliability (can it survive hardware failure, human error, or a regional outage), and security (who can reach the data, and how is it protected at rest and in transit).

In a cloud deployment, much of this is expressed contractually through a Service Level Agreement, and technically through provider features such as redundant replicas, automated backups, and geo-replication. In a local deployment, the same guarantees have to be engineered and funded by the organization itself: backup schedules, standby servers, off-site copies, failover testing, and physical and network security controls. Neither model removes the work — the cloud shifts part of it to the provider under a shared responsibility model, while on-premises keeps all of it in house.

As you watch the videos, look for three things. First, the vocabulary: RTO (recovery time objective), RPO (recovery point objective), high availability versus disaster recovery, failover, and uptime percentages. Second, the mechanisms: what specifically provides redundancy in each model. Third, the division of labor: note each time a task is performed automatically by a provider versus performed manually by a DBA, because that distinction is the heart of the shared responsibility model and of the recommendations you will be asked to make later in this module.
