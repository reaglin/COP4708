Introduction

Choosing between a cloud-hosted and a locally hosted database is rarely just a technical decision — it is a financial one. A local deployment usually means buying servers, storage, and networking gear up front, paying for perpetual or per-core database licenses, and staffing people to patch, back up, and monitor the system. A cloud deployment shifts most of that into a recurring bill that scales with what you consume, but that bill never ends and can grow quickly if workloads are not sized and managed carefully.

This topic gives you the vocabulary and the analytical frame for that comparison: capital expenditure (CapEx) versus operational expenditure (OpEx), hardware and refresh cycles, licensing differences between on-premises and cloud editions, ongoing operational and maintenance labor, and how elasticity changes the cost-efficiency equation. The goal is Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) thinking — accounting for every cost over a multi-year horizon rather than comparing a server price tag to a monthly subscription.

As you watch the videos below, listen for three things: how each cost is classified (one-time versus recurring), who absorbs the cost under each model, and what assumptions drive the answer — workload steadiness, utilization rates, growth rate, and staffing. Those assumptions are usually what decide whether cloud or local comes out cheaper for a given organization, and you will be expected to state them when you make a recommendation.
