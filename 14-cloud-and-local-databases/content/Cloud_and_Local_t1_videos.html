Introduction

A database deployment model describes where a database actually runs and who is responsible for keeping it running. In a locally hosted (on-premises) model, your organization owns or leases the physical servers, storage, and network gear, installs the database software, and handles patching, backups, tuning, and physical security. In a cloud-based model, a provider such as AWS, Microsoft Azure, or Google Cloud supplies the infrastructure and, in managed Database-as-a-Service offerings, much of the routine administration as well.

This distinction matters in COP4708 because almost every design decision later in the module depends on it: how you pay for capacity, how you scale under load, how you achieve availability and disaster recovery, and which security and compliance tasks remain yours. Deployment choice is rarely about which model is technically 'better' — it is about matching infrastructure characteristics to an organization's budget, staffing, regulatory environment, and growth expectations.

As you watch the videos below, keep a running comparison across a few key dimensions: cost structure (upfront capital spending versus ongoing operational spending), scalability, maintenance effort, availability, security, and control. Also pay attention to the division of labor — note each time a speaker mentions a task the provider handles versus one the customer still owns, since that idea becomes the shared responsibility model later in the module.
