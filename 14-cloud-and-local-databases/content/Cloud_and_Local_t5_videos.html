Introduction

Scalability is the ability of a database system to keep meeting performance expectations as data volume, user counts, and query complexity grow. Performance is the related but distinct question of how quickly the system responds under a given load. In this topic we look at both through the lens of deployment model: a locally hosted database is bounded by the hardware you bought and the space, power, and procurement cycle around it, while a cloud database service can often add capacity on demand, sometimes automatically, and bill you for what you use.

This matters in COP4708 because scaling decisions are where the architectural differences between on-premises and cloud deployments become concrete. Vertical scaling (a bigger machine), horizontal scaling (more machines), read replicas, caching, indexing, partitioning, and sharding are techniques available in both worlds, but the cost, speed, and operational burden of applying them differ sharply. Elasticity in the cloud is attractive, yet it is not free: pooled or autoscaling resources introduce their own cost models, limits, and tuning decisions.

As you watch, keep three questions in mind. First, which scaling techniques require application or schema changes and which are purely infrastructure moves? Second, for each technique, what would it take to implement on a server in your own data center versus on a managed cloud service? Third, where does the money go — what are you paying for when you scale up, scale out, or pool resources, and at what point does the trade-off favor one deployment model over the other?

About this video

A short, focused comparison of vertical scaling (adding CPU, memory, or storage to a single machine) and horizontal scaling (distributing work across multiple machines). The video should explain the key differences, the practical ceiling of scaling up, and the complexity that scaling out introduces.

Why this video

This distinction is the core vocabulary for the whole topic: local databases are typically constrained to vertical scaling with hardware you own, while cloud services make horizontal and elastic scaling routine, so a clear four-minute treatment is worth watching before the cloud-specific material.
