Module 5

Advanced Normal Forms: BCNF, 4NF, and 5NF

This lesson extends normalization to Boyce-Codd Normal Form and beyond, covering multivalued dependencies and join dependencies. Students will analyze edge cases where 3NF is insufficient and apply higher normal forms to complex schemas.

    Reading🌐 Web page
    Review of 3NF and Its Limitations

    A reading explaining what 3NF guarantees, the loophole that permits dependencies whose right side is a prime attribute, and the redundancy and update anomalies that therefore survive 3NF — motivating the move to higher normal forms.

  • 📄
    Reading🌐 Web page
    Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)

    A reading defining BCNF as the rule that every nontrivial FD X → Y must have X as a superkey, showing how to spot violations and how BCNF differs from 3NF.

  • 📄
    Reading🌐 Web page
    Comparing 3NF and BCNF

    A side-by-side comparison reading of 3NF and BCNF, covering when the two forms coincide, the classic overlapping-candidate-key gap, and the trade-off between dependency preservation and redundancy removal.

  • 📄
    Reading🌐 Web page
    Multivalued Dependencies and Fourth Normal Form (4NF)

    A reading introducing multivalued dependencies as independent multi-valued facts stored in one table, and defining 4NF along with how to decompose a relation that violates it.

  • 📄
    Reading🌐 Web page
    Join Dependencies and Fifth Normal Form (5NF)

    A reading on join dependencies — relations reconstructible only by joining three or more projections — and 5NF (project-join normal form), including how JDs generalize FDs and MVDs.

  • 📄
    Reading🌐 Web page
    Applying Higher Normal Forms to Complex Schemas

    A practice-oriented reading on taking a messy real-world schema with many attributes, overlapping keys, and vague business rules and systematically normalizing it to BCNF, 4NF, or 5NF.
