Introduction

A join dependency is the most general of the dependency types we study in this course. It says that a relation can be reconstructed exactly by joining several of its projections — no rows lost, no spurious rows added. Functional dependencies and multivalued dependencies are both special cases of this idea; a multivalued dependency is simply a join dependency that splits a relation into exactly two projections. Fifth Normal Form, sometimes called Project-Join Normal Form, requires that every non-trivial join dependency in a relation be implied by its candidate keys.

The practical question 5NF answers is this: when a relation holds a three-way (or wider) association — supplier, part, project; instructor, course, semester; agent, company, product — can it be split into two-column pairs without losing information? Sometimes yes, and the original table carries redundancy that should be removed. Sometimes no, and any two-way split produces a join that invents facts that were never recorded. Learning to tell these cases apart, usually by testing whether the join of the projections reproduces the original rows, is the core skill of this topic.

As you watch, keep three things in view. First, note how each presenter builds the example table and then performs the join to test for a lossless decomposition — that test is what you will be asked to reproduce on assignments. Second, watch for how 5NF is distinguished from 4NF, since the two look similar but rest on different foundations. Third, pay attention to any discussion of cost: 5NF decompositions can require three-way joins to answer ordinary queries, so designers do not always apply it, and you should be able to argue when the trade-off is worth making.
