Project Overview

The client

Sandpiper Cove Animal Shelter is an open-admission county shelter. It takes in about 15 animals a month, mostly dogs and cats, and runs an adoption floor, a foster program, a small clinic and a volunteer program.

The problem

For nine months a records clerk has typed every paper form into one Excel workbook. Each form went into its own tab, and no one set rules for how to enter IDs, names, dates or codes. Staff can't trust the totals, and most of their questions take hours to answer by hand.

What you have

  • The shelter's forms packet (SC-000 to SC-800)
  • The clerk's workbook, Sandpiper_Cove_Shelter_Records_2026.xlsx
  • These interview notes

What you deliver

  • A normalized database design (ERD and table definitions)
  • The workbook data cleaned and loaded
  • A query for every request R-01 to R-36
  • A results report the staff can read
Reporting date. The workbook covers January 2 to September 30, 2026. Wherever a staff member says "today", "right now" or "currently", treat September 30, 2026 as today. "Last month" means September 2026.
DW

Dana Whitfield

Shelter Manager · signs off on outcomes, fees and euthanasia · reports to the county

Dana runs the shelter and writes the monthly report to the County Commission. She also reports twice a year to a national shelter-data program that compares shelters by their intake and outcome numbers.

Every month the county asks how many animals came in and how many went out, and how. It takes me two days, and I'm never sure the numbers match. I count a dog that came back after adoption as two intakes, because it was. Then someone asks how many animals we took in, and I have to explain that's not the same as how many intakes.
The question I get most from the commission is "how many are in the building right now?" I should be able to answer that without walking the kennels.

What we learned

  • An animal keeps the same Animal ID for life. Each time it comes in it gets a new Intake #. Every intake ends with exactly one outcome, unless the animal is still in our care.
  • An animal in a foster home is still in our care. It counts in the population, not as an outcome.
  • "Live release" means the animal left alive: adoption, return to owner or transfer to a partner. Euthanasia and died in care are not live releases.
  • Fees come from the fee schedule on SC-000. An adoption can include more than one fee, plus a promotion discount.
  • Only the Manager can authorize euthanasia, and a veterinarian must perform it.

Requests

  1. R-01For each month, how many animals came in, broken out by species and by how they came in (stray, owner surrender, transfer, seized, returned adoption)?County monthly report.Monthly
  2. R-02For each month, how many animals left, broken out by species and outcome type?The other half of the county report.Monthly
  3. R-03What was our live release rate for the year so far, overall and separately for dogs and cats?Live outcomes divided by all outcomes.Quarterly
  4. R-04How many animals are in our care today, and where is each one? Include the kennel or foster home, and how many days since it came in.Daily
  5. R-05What is the average length of stay for adopted animals, by species? I'd also like to see it split by how the animal came in.Count from the intake date to the outcome date of the same stay.Quarterly
  6. R-06Which animals have been with us more than 45 days and still have no outcome? Longest first.The "long-timer" list for the weekly staff meeting.Weekly
  7. R-07How much fee money did we take in each month? Show adoption fees, reclaim fees and surrender fees separately.Monthly
  8. R-08For each promotion code, how many adoptions used it and how much in fees did we give up?Deciding whether to repeat "Clear the Shelters" next year.Ad hoc
PN

Dr. Priya Nair, DVM

Shelter Veterinarian · with Dr. Marcus Bell (DVM) and Kelly Ortiz (RVT)

The clinic examines every animal at intake, vaccinates it and treats it. Dr. Nair also signs rabies certificates and does most spay and neuter surgeries.

Last spring a manufacturer recalled a vaccine lot. I spent a whole day going through paper to find which animals got it and where they went. If that happens again, I want the list in minutes, with the adopter's phone number.
Kittens and puppies need a booster every three weeks. If nobody catches the due date, they leave without it.

What we learned

  • Every dose is recorded with its vaccine, manufacturer, lot number, date given, who gave it and the next due date.
  • One exam can include several tests. Each test has its own result.
  • A medication order has a start date and a number of days. It is "active" from the start date through the last day.
  • An animal's weight is taken at intake, at every exam, when it goes to foster and when it comes back. Staff want the history, not just the latest weight.
  • Spayed or neutered status can change during a stay. Surgery is done before adoption.

Requests

  1. R-09Which animals still in our care have a vaccine due in the next 14 days? Show the vaccine and the due date.Weekly
  2. R-10If a vaccine lot is recalled, which animals got a dose from that lot, when, and where is each animal now? For adopted animals, include the adopter's name and phone number.Try it with any Rabies lot in the data.On demand
  3. R-11List every animal that tested positive for heartworm, FeLV or FIV, with its test date and what happened to it.Monthly
  4. R-12How many spay and neuter surgeries did each veterinarian do each month?Monthly
  5. R-13Which medications are active today, and for which animals? I want a treatment sheet: animal, kennel, drug, dose, route and how often.Daily
  6. R-14For each animal that went to foster and came back, what was its weight going out and coming back, and how much did it gain?Checking that bottle babies are thriving.Monthly
RS

Rosa Salinas

Animal Care Lead · supervises Animal Care Technicians · assigns kennels

Rosa's team cleans, feeds and moves animals. They are the first to know when the building is full.

When the stray hold ends, the dog moves from the stray side to the adoption floor. When it gets sick, it goes to isolation. Then it goes to a foster home and comes back. By the end, nobody can tell you where it has been.

What we learned

  • Kennels and rooms are listed on SC-000. Each has a code, an area and a capacity. Cat colony rooms hold up to 8 cats; most other units hold one animal.
  • An animal's location is the kennel it was given at intake, until the first logged move. After that it is the "To" location of its latest move.
  • Strays are held 5 days for their owner. Animals with a recent bite history are held 10 days in quarantine.
  • Dogs get a behavior assessment when their hold ends. The color code and placement restrictions guide adoptions.

Requests

  1. R-15For each housing area, how many units are full and how many are open today?"Can we take in the six dogs coming from the seizure tomorrow?"Daily
  2. R-16Give me the full history of one animal in date order: every intake, kennel move, foster stay, exam, vaccine and outcome. Try it with an animal that was returned after adoption.On demand
  3. R-17Which strays had their hold end in the last week of September? Did each one move to the adoption floor, or does it still need a move?Daily
  4. R-18Which dogs in our care right now are coded Orange or Red, and what restrictions do they have?Only experienced staff may handle them.Daily
TG

Tamara Greene

Adoption Counselor · with Luis Pena · reviews applications, completes adoptions, handles returns

Tamara matches adopters with animals. She reads the application, calls the landlord and the vet, and signs the contract.

People come in asking for "a small dog that's good with kids." I walk the floor and guess. I want to type in what they need and get a list.
The same family adopts from us, volunteers on Saturdays and donates at Christmas. Right now they're in three places under three spellings. I'd love to know who our best friends are.

What we learned

  • A person can submit many applications, and an animal can get many. Only one application per animal leads to an adoption.
  • Applications are Pending, Approved, Denied or Withdrawn. Every adoption must point to an approved application.
  • An adopted animal can come back. The return closes that adoption, and the animal gets a new intake. Returns within 14 days get a store credit.
  • Staff call adopters 3 days and 30 days after an adoption. Both calls must be recorded.
  • The same person can be an adopter, finder, owner, volunteer, foster or donor. Staff want each person to be one person.

Requests

  1. R-19Show me every dog available for adoption today that weighs under 25 pounds, is coded Green or Yellow, and has no "no children" restriction.The search should work for other combinations too.On demand
  2. R-20How many applications do we have in each status? List every Pending application that is more than 7 days old.Weekly
  3. R-21Which animals got the most applications? Show each animal with its number of applications and who finally adopted it.Monthly
  4. R-22What share of adoptions came back? Break it down by species and by return reason, and tell me how many came back within 14 days.Quarterly
  5. R-23Which adoptions are missing a 3-day or 30-day follow-up call, and which counselor did the adoption?Weekly
  6. R-24Has anyone who was denied an application applied again or adopted later? Show the earlier denial reason.On demand
  7. R-25List everyone who has more than one role with us, such as an adopter who also volunteers or donates, and show what each person has done.Annual
EB

Officer Erik Brandt

Animal Control Officer · with Officer Carla Sims · field pickups, seizures, bite cases

Animal Control answers calls about loose animals, cruelty and bites. They bring animals in and file the bite reports the county health department needs.

I want to know where the strays come from. If one neighborhood keeps sending us litters, that's where we run a spay/neuter event.
Microchips are the fastest way home. I'd like numbers I can show the commission.

What we learned

  • Every stray has a found location. Field pickups also have the officer who brought the animal in.
  • One incident can involve more than one animal, for example a dog fight. Each incident is about one injured person at most.
  • A human bite starts a 10-day quarantine and must be reported to the county health department.
  • Lost and found reports from the public are compared with animals in our care. A report can be matched to an animal.

Requests

  1. R-26How many strays came from each city, and which cities sent us the most cats?Quarterly
  2. R-27What percentage of stray dogs went home to their owners? Compare dogs that had a microchip at intake with dogs that didn't.Quarterly
  3. R-28List every bite and incident: date, animals involved, injured person, severity, whether it was reported to the health department, and the quarantine dates.Monthly
  4. R-29For each open "lost" report, which animals in our care are the same species and came in within 7 days of the date the pet was lost?The possible-match list for the front desk.Daily
MR

Megan Ruiz

Volunteer & Foster Coordinator · runs orientation and training, approves foster homes

Megan manages about 30 volunteers and 11 foster homes. She signs court-ordered service letters and tracks who is trained for what.

A judge's clerk called asking for a volunteer's total hours. I added up sign-in sheets with a calculator. Half the time people forget to sign out.
When intake brings in four bottle kittens at 5 pm, I need to know which foster homes have room and are approved for bottle babies.

What we learned

  • Volunteers need orientation before their first shift. Dog Level 2 dogs may only be walked by Level 2 volunteers.
  • A shift's hours are the time between sign-in and sign-out. A shift with no sign-out has no hours until someone fixes it.
  • On a shift, a volunteer can work with several animals.
  • Each foster home has a maximum number of animals and a list of the foster types it is approved for. A placement is open until the animal comes back.

Requests

  1. R-30How many hours did each volunteer work each month? Show the top 10 volunteers for the year.Monthly
  2. R-31For volunteers doing court-ordered or school hours, how many hours has each one completed, and when was their last shift?On demand
  3. R-32Which active volunteers haven't worked a shift in the last 60 days? Include their phone and email so I can reach out.Monthly
  4. R-33Which dogs in our care did no volunteer work with during the last week of September?So those dogs get walked first.Weekly
  5. R-34For each active foster home, how many animals does it have now, how many can it take, and what is it approved for? Only show homes with room.Daily
DW

Dana Whitfield, second interview

Donations & fundraising · works with the shelter's nonprofit foundation

Dana met with us a second time to talk about donations. Gifts of money and goods both come through the front desk. Donors can give to a fund or sponsor a specific animal's adoption fee.

Donors who get a thank-you within a week give again. Donors who never hear from us don't. I want to know who we've missed.

What we learned

  • A donation comes from a person, a business, or both (a business with a contact person). Some gifts are anonymous.
  • One in-kind donation can include many items, each with a category and quantity. The shelter does not put a value on goods.
  • A sponsorship gift is tied to one animal.

Requests

  1. R-35What is the total cash given by designation and by month? Which donors gave more than once?Monthly
  2. R-36Which donations still haven't had a thank-you sent? Include the donor's contact information and the gift.Weekly

Open Questions

These came up in the interviews and still need a decision. Write down how your design handles each one and why.

  • Some workbook records point to an Animal ID that doesn't exist, or leave out the Intake # or Application #. Staff want them kept, not deleted. How will you load them?
  • Several dates were typed without a year, for example "Mar 04". What rule will you use to fill in the year?
  • Staff sometimes recorded a vaccine, a test or a medication in a notes column instead of its own column. Should the database have a general notes field, or should these move into their proper tables?
  • The county may change the fee schedule in January. How will last year's adoptions keep the fees that were actually charged?
  • Two records may describe the same person with a different name and the same phone number. Who decides they are one person, and how is that decision recorded?
