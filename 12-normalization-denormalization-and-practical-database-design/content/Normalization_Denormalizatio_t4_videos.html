Introduction

Normalization gives us schemas that store each fact exactly once, which protects data integrity and makes updates simple. Denormalization is the deliberate, selective reversal of some of those decisions in order to make certain queries faster. The key word is deliberate: you denormalize after you have a normalized design, after you know your workload, and after you have measured that joins or repeated computation are actually a bottleneck.

This topic surveys the standard patterns. Storing a precomputed or derived value (an order total, an age, a full name) avoids recomputing it on every read. Merging tables, or table collapsing, folds a one-to-one or rarely-separate relationship into a single table to eliminate a join. Adding a redundant column copies an attribute from a parent table into a child so a lookup join disappears. Summary and aggregate tables materialize counts, sums, and rollups for reporting. Vertical partitioning splits a wide table by column usage, which is technically the opposite move but belongs to the same family of physical-design trade-offs.

As you watch, keep a running ledger of costs. Every redundant copy creates an update anomaly risk, and you must name the mechanism that keeps the copy correct: application code, a trigger, a materialized view refresh, or a scheduled batch job. Also notice how each video frames the read/write balance, since the right pattern for a read-heavy analytics workload is usually wrong for a write-heavy transactional one.
