Introduction

Up to this point you have learned the vocabulary of ER modeling: entities, attributes, keys, relationships, cardinality, and participation. This topic is where that vocabulary gets put to work. Given a messy, informal description of a business — a library, a clinic, an online store — your job is to read it carefully, decide what the system actually needs to keep track of, and turn that into a diagram precise enough that someone else could build tables from it.

The work usually follows a repeatable path. You analyze the requirements and underline the nouns and verbs that matter, propose a candidate list of entities, decide which details belong to which entity as attributes, name the relationships that connect entities, and then pin down how many of one relate to how many of the other and whether participation is optional or mandatory. Only then do you draw the full diagram, and even then you are not finished: a good designer re-reads the original description and checks the model against it, looking for duplicated data, missing entities, or relationships that were assumed rather than stated.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to the reasoning rather than just the finished picture. Notice how a noun in a sentence becomes either an entity or an attribute depending on how much detail the business needs, how a many-to-many relationship is handled, and what kinds of errors experienced designers watch out for. Try pausing each video after the requirements are stated and sketching your own diagram before seeing the presenter's version — comparing the two is where most of the learning happens.
