Introduction

Data modeling is the practice of describing the data an organization cares about — the things it tracks, the facts it stores about those things, and the connections between them — before any tables are created in a database system. Think of it as the architectural drawing that comes before construction: it lets you reason about structure, catch problems, and agree with stakeholders on meaning while changes are still cheap.

In this course, data modeling is the bridge between a written problem description and a working relational database. It matters because design decisions made at the modeling stage ripple through everything that follows: query complexity, data integrity, redundancy, and how easily the system can grow. The topic also introduces the idea of levels of abstraction — conceptual, logical, and physical — which gives you a vocabulary for talking about the same data at different degrees of detail and independence from any particular DBMS.

As you watch the videos below, look for three things. First, note how each presenter defines a model and what they say it is for. Second, pay close attention to where the boundary falls between conceptual, logical, and physical descriptions, and what information is added at each step. Third, watch how modeling fits into an overall design workflow — where requirements come in and where implementation begins.
