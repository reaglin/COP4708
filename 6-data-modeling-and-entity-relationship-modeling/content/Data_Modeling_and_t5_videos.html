Introduction

An entity-relationship (ER) diagram is a picture of the data a system needs to store. It shows the things we care about (entities), the facts we record about them (attributes), and how those things are connected (relationships). Because the diagram is built before any tables exist, it lets you and your stakeholders agree on the structure of the data while changes are still cheap.

In this module the ER diagram is the bridge between a written problem description and an actual database. You will practice reading a scenario, pulling out the nouns that become entities, deciding which details belong as attributes, drawing the relationships, and then labeling each relationship with cardinality (one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many) and participation (whether every instance must take part). Notation matters here: Chen-style and crow's foot notation express the same ideas with different symbols, so be sure you can read both and stay consistent within a single diagram.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to three things. First, how the presenter decides what becomes an entity versus what becomes an attribute. Second, how cardinality symbols are placed and read at each end of a relationship line. Third, how many-to-many relationships are handled, since that choice is where conceptual diagrams begin to turn into something implementable. Try pausing and sketching the examples yourself before the answer appears on screen.
