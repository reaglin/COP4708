One Database to Rule Them All? Matching the Model to the Mission

Prompt

Databases sit behind nearly everything you use: online shopping, hospital records, bank transfers, social feeds, your LMS, government services, and parcel tracking. But these industries want very different things from their data. Some cannot tolerate a single inconsistent record. Others accept small imperfections in exchange for serving enormous numbers of users at once. When an industry's needs pull in different directions — strict integrity versus massive scale, fixed structure versus flexible structure — what should drive the choice of database model, and what is that trade-off worth?

Your initial post (150–250 words), due early in the week

Choose one industry from this module: e-commerce and retail, healthcare, banking and financial services, social media and content platforms, education and learning management systems, government and public services, or transportation and logistics. Put the industry name in your post title so the class covers a range.

In your post:

  • Describe what kind of data this industry handles and what relationships exist between the pieces of data.
  • Identify the one criterion this industry cannot compromise on — schema flexibility, query capability, scalability, or data integrity — and explain why, using a concrete situation as an example.
  • Name what this industry can afford to compromise on, and what the consequence would be.
  • End with one question or uncertainty you want classmates to take on.

Your replies — two, due by the end of the week

At least one reply must go to someone who chose a different industry from yours. A good reply does more than agree: it quotes the specific claim you are responding to, then adds a counter-example, a criterion the author left out, or a case from your own industry that tests their reasoning. Replies that end with a question keep the thread alive.

Assessment: Posts are assessed on whether you take a clear, reasoned position grounded in the module's comparison criteria, support it with a concrete industry example, and engage substantively with a classmate's actual argument.
