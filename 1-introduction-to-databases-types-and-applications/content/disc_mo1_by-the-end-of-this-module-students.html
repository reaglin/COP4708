Naming the Parts: Why Database Vocabulary Is Worth Arguing About

Prompt: Of the six key database terms in this module — record, field, table, primary key, foreign key, and schema — which one is hardest to define precisely in your own words, and what makes it hard?

Your initial post (150–250 words), early in the week, should include:

  • Your definition, in your own words, of all six terms (one short line each — these can be brisk).
  • Your pick for the hardest term, and a clear explanation of what makes it slippery: is it that everyday language uses the word differently, that it overlaps with another term, or that you can state it but not yet apply it?
  • One concrete example from a collection of information you know — a club roster, a playlist, a recipe box, a league table, a payroll sheet. Point to what counts as a table, a field, a record, and at least one key in it.
  • One honest question you still have about the vocabulary.

Replies: Post two replies by the end of the week, to classmates who picked a different hardest term than yours. A good reply quotes the specific sentence you're responding to, then does one of three things: tests their definition against a new example, offers a sharper wording and says why it's sharper, or answers the open question they posed. Replies that only say "I agree" or "great post" don't count — add a reason, an example, or a challenge.

Assessment: Posts are assessed on whether you use all six terms accurately and distinctly, support your claims with a concrete example, and engage substantively with at least two classmates.
